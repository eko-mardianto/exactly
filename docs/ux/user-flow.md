# User Flow

## Purpose

User Flow defines the actions users take to move through Exactly while pursuing a specific goal.

Rather than describing how information is organized or how users experience the broader journey, this document focuses on the sequence of actions, choices, and outcomes that enable users to progress through the product.

It provides the foundation for navigation, screen structure, and interaction design.

## Flow Principles

Flow Principles define the enduring principles that guide how user actions should be structured throughout Exactly.

These principles ensure that user flows remain understandable, purposeful, and aligned with the product's goal of strengthening human judgment.

---

### Purpose Before Action

Users should understand why an action is necessary before being asked to perform it.

Actions should contribute meaningfully to the user's goal rather than exist only because the interface requires them.

---

### Understanding Before Progress

Users should have enough information to understand a meaningful step before being encouraged to continue.

The flow should not prioritize completion at the expense of understanding.

---

### Meaningful Choice

When users are required to make a choice, the available options should be understandable and relevant to the decision.

Choices should help users exercise judgment rather than create unnecessary friction.

---

### Clear Progression

Each action should lead naturally toward the user's intended outcome.

Users should be able to understand where they are, what they are doing, and what the next meaningful step is.

---

### Recoverable Paths

Users should be able to recover from mistakes, reconsider choices, or return to previous steps when appropriate.

A user flow should not make every decision irreversible unless there is a clear reason to do so.

---

User flows should help users make meaningful progress without turning completion into the primary objective.

---

## Core User Flow

The core user flow represents the primary path through which a user moves from an initial question toward an informed decision.

The core flow is:

Question

↓

Explore

↓

Understand

↓

Evaluate

↓

Decide

↓

Reflect

The flow begins when a user identifies something they need to understand.

The user then explores relevant information, develops understanding through evidence and reasoning, evaluates what they have learned, applies their own judgment, and may later reflect on the decision or outcome.

---

## Flow Stages

### Question

The user identifies a question, uncertainty, problem, or decision that requires better understanding.

**User action:**

- Define or communicate what they want to understand.

**Expected outcome:**

- A clear starting point for exploration.

---

### Explore

The user examines relevant information, evidence, sources, and perspectives.

**User action:**

- Review available information.
- Explore relevant context.
- Identify information that may help answer the question.

**Expected outcome:**

- A broader understanding of the available information.

---

### Understand

The user connects information, evidence, explanations, and reasoning.

**User action:**

- Examine how information relates.
- Review explanations.
- Build an understanding of the subject.

**Expected outcome:**

- A coherent understanding of the issue.

---

### Evaluate

The user assesses the strength, relevance, and reliability of the information.

**User action:**

- Compare evidence.
- Examine supporting reasoning.
- Identify uncertainty or limitations.
- Determine what information can reasonably be trusted.

**Expected outcome:**

- A more informed basis for judgment.

---

### Decide

The user applies their own judgment to the situation.

**User action:**

- Consider the information and reasoning.
- Weigh relevant factors.
- Make their own decision.

**Expected outcome:**

- A decision supported by understanding.

Exactly supports this process without making the decision on behalf of the user.

---

### Reflect

The user reviews the reasoning, decision, or outcome when reflection is relevant.

**User action:**

- Revisit previous understanding.
- Review the reasoning behind the decision.
- Learn from the outcome.
- Update their understanding when new information becomes available.

**Expected outcome:**

- Improved understanding that can support future decisions.

---

## Decision Points

Decision Points represent moments where the user must determine how to proceed.

Examples include:

### Continue Exploring

The user may determine that they do not yet have enough information to move toward evaluation.

```text
Not enough understanding
        ↓
Continue Exploring
        ↓
Explore
```
