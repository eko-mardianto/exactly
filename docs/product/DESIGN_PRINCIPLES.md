# Design Principles

## Purpose

Design Principles define the enduring principles that guide how Exactly should be designed and experienced.

Rather than prescribing specific interface solutions, these principles establish the standards that should influence future product, interaction, and user experience decisions.

As the product evolves, these principles help ensure consistency between the product's purpose, positioning, and user experience.

---

## Understanding Before Simplicity

Design should help users understand before attempting to simplify.

Simplicity is valuable only when it preserves meaning. When simplicity removes important context or reasoning, understanding should always take priority.

Every interface should make complex information easier to understand without hiding the information users need to make informed decisions.

---

**Design should reduce complexity without reducing understanding.**

---

## Explain Before Showing

Design should help users understand the reasoning behind information rather than simply presenting results.

Interfaces should provide sufficient context, evidence, and explanations so users can follow how conclusions are reached instead of accepting them without question.

Whenever possible, the product should make reasoning visible rather than hiding it behind outputs or recommendations.

---

**Design should reveal reasoning, not just results.**

---

## Evidence Before Conclusions

Design should encourage users to evaluate evidence before accepting conclusions.

Information should be presented in a way that makes supporting evidence accessible, understandable, and connected to every important conclusion.

Users should always be able to see what supports a conclusion before deciding whether to trust it.

---

**Design should make evidence visible before conclusions become convincing.**

---

## Support Human Judgment

Design should empower users to make their own decisions rather than encouraging passive acceptance of recommendations.

Interfaces should present information, reasoning, and evidence in ways that strengthen users' confidence without reducing their responsibility for the final decision.

The product should guide thoughtful decision-making while always leaving the final judgment to the user.

---

**Design should strengthen judgment, never replace it.**

---

## Clarity Through Consistency

Design should remain consistent so users can focus on understanding information rather than learning different interaction patterns.

Consistent language, visual presentation, interaction behavior, and information structure help reduce unnecessary cognitive effort and improve confidence throughout the user experience.

Consistency should always serve clarity, making the product easier to learn, easier to navigate, and easier to trust.

---

**Design should build trust through consistency.**

---

## Design Principles Statement

These Design Principles define the enduring standards that guide how Exactly should be designed, experienced, and evolved.

They are not tied to specific interfaces, technologies, or implementation decisions.

Instead, they provide a consistent foundation for every product, design, and engineering decision, ensuring that future development remains aligned with Exactly's purpose, positioning, and philosophy.

As the product grows, individual features, visual designs, and technologies may change.

These principles should remain stable and continue to shape how users experience Exactly.
