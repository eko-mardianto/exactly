# Chapter 1 — Purpose

The Brand Language Guide defines how Exactly communicates across every touchpoint.

Its purpose is to ensure that every message reflects the same clarity, reasoning, and respect for human judgment.

This guide establishes a shared communication standard across the organization, helping maintain a consistent brand voice while allowing communication to adapt naturally across different audiences, products, and languages.

It serves as the primary communication standard for all content created on behalf of Exactly, including product interfaces, documentation, marketing materials, customer support, educational content, and all other forms of brand communication.

---

## Canonical Copy (English)

English is the canonical language of Exactly.

All branding, messaging, and communication guidelines are created and maintained in English as the single source of truth.

When communicating in other languages, the objective is not to translate words literally, but to preserve the original meaning, intent, tone, and philosophy of the brand.

---

## Bahasa Indonesia

Bahasa Indonesia merupakan versi resmi Exactly untuk audiens di Indonesia.

Seluruh komunikasi dalam Bahasa Indonesia harus mempertahankan makna, tujuan, nada, dan filosofi dari versi kanonik berbahasa Inggris. Penyesuaian bahasa dilakukan agar pesan Exactly terasa alami bagi pembaca Indonesia tanpa mengubah identitas maupun maksud aslinya.

---

## Guiding Principle

**Canonical Copy defines what Exactly means.**

**Each language adapts how Exactly is naturally understood by its audience without changing the brand's meaning or philosophy.**

# Chapter 2 — Brand Voice

Brand Voice defines the personality of Exactly in every form of communication.

Regardless of the audience, platform, or language, every message should sound unmistakably like Exactly. A consistent voice builds familiarity, trust, and credibility over time.

The characteristics below serve as the communication standard for everyone creating content on behalf of Exactly.

---

## Our Voice Is

### Clear

We communicate in a way that is easy to understand without sacrificing accuracy.

Complex ideas should become simpler—not oversimplified.

---

### Rational

We communicate through reasoning, evidence, and context rather than assumptions or emotions.

Our conclusions should always be explainable.

---

### Curious

We encourage exploration, thoughtful questions, and continuous learning.

We believe understanding grows through curiosity, not certainty.

---

### Respectful

We respect the reader's intelligence, experience, and ability to make decisions.

We explain ideas without being patronizing, dismissive, or judgmental.

---

### Confident

Our confidence comes from understanding and evidence—not from certainty or exaggeration.

We communicate with confidence while remaining intellectually honest.

---

## Our Voice Is Never

### Arrogant

We never present ourselves as having all the answers.

We acknowledge uncertainty whenever it exists.

---

### Manipulative

We never pressure people through fear, urgency, or emotional persuasion.

People deserve understanding—not coercion.

---

### Sensational

We never exaggerate headlines, claims, or outcomes simply to attract attention.

Accuracy always comes before attention.

---

### Absolute

We avoid presenting opinions as unquestionable facts.

We recognize that evidence can evolve and different perspectives may exist.

---

### Complicated

We never use unnecessary jargon or complexity to appear more knowledgeable.

If something can be explained more clearly, it should be.

---

## Voice in Practice

### Instead of

> "Buy this stock now."

### We say

> "Here's why this opportunity may deserve your attention."

---

### Instead of

> "This stock will definitely rise."

### We say

> "Current evidence suggests improving momentum, but additional factors should be considered before making a decision."

---

### Instead of

> "Trust our analysis."

### We say

> "Here's the reasoning behind our analysis."

---

### Instead of

> "Our system knows the answer."

### We say

> "Our analysis provides evidence to support your decision."

---

## Guiding Principle

**A consistent voice is not about sounding identical.**

**It is about expressing the same values, reasoning, and respect in every conversation.**

**Every message should help people understand before they decide.**

# Chapter 3 — Communication Principles

Communication Principles define how ideas should be communicated, regardless of the platform, audience, or format.

These principles ensure that every message reflects Exactly's philosophy, maintains consistency across all communication, and helps people build understanding before making decisions.

---

## 1. Explain Before Concluding

Begin with explanation before presenting conclusions.

Whenever possible, provide the reasoning, evidence, or context first, allowing readers to understand how a conclusion is reached rather than simply accepting it.

Understanding should lead to conclusions—not the other way around.

---

## 2. Evidence Before Opinions

Support every meaningful claim with evidence, reasoning, or observable facts.

Opinions without explanation reduce trust. Whenever evidence is limited or uncertain, communicate that uncertainty honestly.

Readers should understand not only _what_ is being communicated, but also _why_ it is believed to be true.

---

## 3. Context Before Recommendation

Recommendations should never exist without context.

Before suggesting an action, provide the surrounding information that helps readers evaluate the situation for themselves.

The objective is to improve judgment—not replace it.

---

## 4. Respect the Reader's Judgment

Respect every reader's ability to think and decide independently.

Communicate with guidance rather than authority.

Avoid language that pressures, manipulates, or assumes superiority.

Exactly informs decisions.
The decision always belongs to the user.

---

## 5. Simplicity Without Losing Meaning

Communicate with clarity, not complexity.

Use simple language whenever possible, while preserving the accuracy and depth of the original idea.

A message becomes more valuable when more people can understand it without sacrificing its meaning.

---

## 6. Build Confidence Through Understanding

The purpose of communication is not to make people agree.

The purpose is to help people understand well enough to make confident decisions.

Confidence built on understanding is more sustainable than confidence built on persuasion.

---

## Guiding Principle

**We do not communicate to impress people.**

**We communicate to help people understand.**

**When people understand better, they decide with greater confidence.**

# Chapter 4 — Tone of Voice

Tone of Voice defines how Exactly adapts its communication to different situations while preserving the same Brand Voice.

Regardless of the context, our voice remains consistent. Only the tone changes to suit the audience, purpose, and circumstances.

---

### Purpose

Help people learn and build understanding.

### Tone

- Clear
- Curious
- Patient
- Encouraging

### Example

Instead of:

> "This concept is easy."

We say:

> "Let's break this concept down step by step."

---

## Market Analysis

### Purpose

Present information objectively so readers can evaluate it independently.

### Tone

- Rational
- Objective
- Calm
- Evidence-based

### Example

Instead of:

> "This stock is a strong buy."

We say:

> "Current evidence suggests improving momentum, but investors should consider additional factors before making a decision."

---

## Product Interface

### Purpose

Help users complete tasks with confidence.

### Tone

- Simple
- Friendly
- Direct
- Helpful

### Example

Instead of:

> "Invalid input."

We say:

> "Please check the highlighted fields and try again."

---

## Success Messages

### Purpose

Acknowledge successful actions without unnecessary excitement.

### Tone

- Positive
- Confident
- Humble

### Example

Instead of:

> "Congratulations! Amazing!"

We say:

> "Your changes have been saved successfully."

---

## Warnings & Risk

### Purpose

Communicate risks without creating fear.

### Tone

- Serious
- Calm
- Objective

### Example

Instead of:

> "Danger! You could lose everything!"

> "This decision involves risk. Please review the available information before continuing."

### Purpose

Help users recover quickly when something goes wrong.

### Tone

- Calm
- Helpful
- Reassuring

- Patient

### Purpose

Encourage conversation, curiosity, and knowledge sharing.

### Tone

- Friendly
- Curious
- Respectful
- Professional

---

**Tone adapts to the situation. Voice never changes.**

**Every message should still sound unmistakably like Exactly.**

# Chapter 5 — Writing Principles

Writing Principles define how Exactly writes across every product, service, and communication channel.

These principles ensure that every piece of writing is clear, consistent, and easy to understand while remaining faithful to Exactly's Brand Voice and Communication Principles.

---

## 1. Start with the Main Idea

Present the most important information first.

Readers should understand the purpose of a message as early as possible before reading supporting details.

### Example

Instead of:

> Before discussing this topic, it is important to understand...

We say:

> This guide explains how...

---

## 2. Keep It Simple

Choose the simplest words and sentence structures that accurately communicate the intended meaning.

Simple writing makes complex ideas easier to understand.

Complex ideas are acceptable.

Complex writing is not.

### Example

Instead of:

> Utilize

We say:

> Use

---

## 3. One Idea per Paragraph

Each paragraph should communicate a single primary idea.

If a new idea begins, start a new paragraph.

This improves readability and helps readers follow the flow of information.

---

## 4. Be Specific

Prefer concrete information over vague statements.

Whenever possible, use observable facts, numbers, examples, or clear descriptions.

### Example

Instead of:

> Many investors are interested.

We say:

> Institutional ownership increased by 12% over the past quarter.

---

## 5. Explain Technical Terms

Do not assume readers share the same level of knowledge.

When technical terms are necessary, explain them before introducing abbreviations or specialized vocabulary.

### Example

Instead of:

> RSI indicates...

We say:

> The Relative Strength Index (RSI) measures price momentum...

---

## 6. Remove Unnecessary Words

Every sentence should contribute value.

Avoid repetition, filler words, and unnecessary qualifiers that do not improve understanding.

If a sentence can be shortened without changing its meaning, shorten it.

---

## 7. Write Honestly

Communicate confidence when supported by evidence.

Communicate uncertainty when evidence is incomplete.

Never exaggerate certainty or hide meaningful limitations.

Trust grows through transparency.

---

## 8. End Naturally

Conclude by reinforcing understanding rather than forcing action.

Whenever appropriate, leave readers with greater clarity instead of stronger pressure.

### Example

Instead of:

> Buy now before it's too late.

We say:

> Consider how this information fits your own investment objectives before making a decision.

---

## Guiding Principle

**Write to make understanding effortless.**

# Chapter 6 — Vocabulary

Vocabulary defines the language Exactly chooses to communicate ideas consistently across every product, service, and communication channel.

Rather than limiting creativity, these guidelines establish a shared vocabulary that reinforces Exactly's philosophy, improves clarity, and builds familiarity over time.

---

## 1. Choose Words People Naturally Understand

Prefer everyday language over unnecessarily technical or formal words.

Communication becomes stronger when readers understand the message without additional effort.

### Example

Instead of:

> Utilize

We say:

> Use

---

## 2. Prefer Understanding Over Certainty

Choose words that encourage understanding rather than absolute conclusions.

Whenever possible, describe observations instead of making definitive claims.

### Example

Instead of:

> This will happen.

We say:

> Current evidence suggests...

---

## 3. Prefer Evidence Over Assumptions

Choose words that reflect observable information, reasoning, and transparency.

Avoid language that asks readers to believe without explanation.

### Example

Instead of:

> Trust us.

We say:

> Here's why.

---

## 4. Use Consistent Terminology

Use the same terms consistently throughout the product and documentation.

Consistency helps readers build familiarity with Exactly's language.

### Example

Always use:

- Brand Voice
- Communication Principles
- Canonical Copy

Avoid alternating between multiple terms that describe the same concept.

---

## 5. Avoid Unnecessary Superlatives

Avoid words that exaggerate quality, certainty, or importance unless they can be objectively supported.

### Example

Instead of:

> The best solution.

We say:

> A suitable solution.

---

## 6. Avoid Manipulative Language

Do not use words that create unnecessary fear, urgency, or emotional pressure.

Exactly informs decisions rather than persuades them.

### Example

Instead of:

> Act now before it's too late.

We say:

> Consider this information before making your decision.

---

## 7. Use Human Language

Write as if speaking to an intelligent person—not writing for a machine.

Prefer natural, conversational language while maintaining professionalism.

Readers should feel respected, not lectured.

---

## Preferred Expressions

Whenever appropriate, prefer expressions such as:

- Current evidence suggests...
- Based on available information...
- Here's why...
- Let's understand...
- Consider...
- It depends on...
- This may indicate...

---

## Expressions to Avoid

Avoid expressions such as:

- Obviously...
- Everyone knows...
- Trust us.
- Guaranteed.
- Secret.
- Risk-free.
- Perfect.
- You must...
- Without a doubt.

---

## Guiding Principle

**Choose words that help people think more clearly, not feel more certain.**

# Chapter 7 — Grammar & Style

Grammar & Style define the editorial standards used across all Exactly communication.

These standards ensure that every piece of content maintains consistency, readability, and professionalism regardless of the author, platform, or format.

---

## 1. Write in Plain English

Use clear, natural English.

Avoid unnecessarily formal, academic, or complicated sentence structures.

Write for understanding rather than sophistication.

---

## 2. Use Active Voice

Prefer active voice whenever possible.

Active sentences are generally shorter, clearer, and easier to understand.

### Example

Instead of:

> The report was generated by the system.

We say:

> The system generated the report.

---

## 3. Keep Sentences Concise

Aim for concise sentences.

Break long sentences into shorter ones when doing so improves readability.

---

## 4. Use Lists for Readability

Use bullet points or numbered lists when presenting multiple related ideas.

Lists help readers scan information more efficiently.

---

## 5. Use Headings Consistently

Organize content with clear heading levels.

Use headings to guide readers through the structure of a document.

Avoid skipping heading levels.

---

## 6. Format for Readability

Use whitespace intentionally.

Separate sections clearly.

Avoid large blocks of uninterrupted text.

Readable formatting improves comprehension.

---

## 7. Use Consistent Capitalization

Capitalize official product names, document titles, and defined terminology consistently.

### Examples

- Exactly
- Brand Foundation
- Brand Voice
- Communication Principles
- Writing Principles
- Canonical Copy

---

## 8. Capitalize Defined Terms

Defined terms established by Exactly should always be capitalized when referring to their official meaning.

This distinguishes official concepts from their generic usage and helps maintain consistency across all documentation.

### Examples

Correct

- Brand Foundation
- Brand Philosophy
- Brand Voice
- Communication Principles
- Tone of Voice
- Writing Principles
- Canonical Copy
- Official Adaptation

Incorrect

- brand foundation
- brand voice
- communication principles
- canonical copy
- official adaptation

---

## 9. Use Punctuation to Improve Clarity

Punctuation should improve readability rather than add complexity.

Prefer simple punctuation that supports natural reading.

Avoid excessive punctuation marks.

### Instead of

> Amazing!!!

We write:

> Great.

---

## 10. Be Consistent

When multiple acceptable writing styles exist, choose one and use it consistently throughout the document.

Consistency is more important than personal preference.

---

## Guiding Principle

**Consistency makes communication easier to understand.**

# Chapter 8 — Bahasa Indonesia

Bahasa Indonesia is the **Official Adaptation** of Exactly's **Canonical Copy** for Indonesian audiences.

The purpose of this chapter is not to translate English literally, but to preserve the meaning, intent, tone, and philosophy of Exactly in natural Bahasa Indonesia.

Every **Official Adaptation** should feel as though it was originally written in Bahasa Indonesia while remaining faithful to the **Canonical Copy**.

---

## 1. Preserve Meaning, Not Words

Translate ideas rather than individual words.

When a literal translation sounds unnatural, prioritize the intended meaning over the original wording.

### Example

Canonical Copy

> Help people understand before they decide.

Official Adaptation

> Membantu orang memahami sebelum mengambil keputusan.

Not

> Membantu orang mengerti sebelum mereka memutuskan.

---

## 2. Write Naturally

Write in language that feels natural to Indonesian readers.

Avoid sentence structures that sound like direct translations from English.

The goal is to communicate clearly—not to mirror English grammar.

### Example

Instead of

> Kami ingin membuat Anda memahami...

We write

> Kami ingin membantu Anda memahami...

---

## 3. Preserve the Brand Voice

Language may change.

Brand Voice must remain consistent.

Every **Official Adaptation** should continue to sound:

- Clear
- Rational
- Curious
- Respectful
- Confident

---

## 4. Adapt Cultural Expressions

Not every English expression has a natural Indonesian equivalent.

Adapt expressions according to Indonesian language and culture while preserving the original intent.

Meaning always takes priority over literal wording.

---

## 5. Standard Terminology

The following terminology is standardized across Exactly's English and Bahasa Indonesia communication.

| English                  | Bahasa Indonesia         |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Canonical Copy           | Naskah Kanonik           |
| Official Adaptation      | Adaptasi Resmi           |
| Brand Foundation         | Brand Foundation         |
| Brand Philosophy         | Brand Philosophy         |
| Brand Voice              | Brand Voice              |
| Communication Principles | Communication Principles |
| Tone of Voice            | Tone of Voice            |
| Writing Principles       | Writing Principles       |

Brand-specific terminology remains in English unless an official Bahasa Indonesia equivalent has been established.

---

## 6. Maintain Consistency

Once an official term has been established, use it consistently across all products, documentation, and communication.

Avoid using multiple Indonesian translations for the same concept.

Consistency strengthens recognition and reduces ambiguity.

---

## 7. Prioritize Reader Experience

Readers should never feel they are reading a translation.

Every **Official Adaptation** should read naturally, clearly, and confidently—as if it were originally written in Bahasa Indonesia.

---

## Guiding Principle

**Adapt the language. Preserve the meaning.**
